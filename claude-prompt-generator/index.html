When to use Claude-optimized prompts

Use Claude-optimized prompts for long-document analysis, nuanced writing, careful multi-step reasoning, coding with large context, and tasks where tone and constraints genuinely matter.

Claude strengths to prompt around

Long-context handling

Strong at working across large documents or codebases without losing track of earlier details.

Careful writing

Nuanced writing with good instruction-following on tone, constraints, and audience.

Structured reasoning

Reasons well step by step when a prompt asks it to show its work.

Handles ambiguity

Will ask clarifying questions when prompted to, rather than guessing silently.

Bad prompt vs. structured prompt

Bad prompt:

“Summarize this document.”

Structured:

Role: You are a research analyst.
Action: Read the attached report and produce a 5-bullet executive summary.
Context: Audience is a non-technical VP. Prioritize business impact over methodology.
Example: Flag any claims that lack supporting data, similar to how an analyst would annotate a memo before forwarding it.

What you get back

A 5-bullet summary a VP could act on without a follow-up question — business impact first, methodology skipped unless it changes the conclusion, and any unsupported claims clearly flagged.
