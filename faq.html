Prompt frameworks explained

What is the Standard (ORACLE) framework?

ORACLE stands for Objective, Role, Action, Context, Layout/Length, Example. It's a well-rounded, default structure that works for most everyday prompts.

What is the Reasoning framework?

Built for tasks that require logical, step-by-step thinking. Best when you need the AI to work through a problem, analyse something, or explain its reasoning before answering.

What is the RACE framework?

RACE stands for Role, Action, Context, Example. It keeps prompts direct and goal-focused — good for content creation and tasks where you want a clear output fast.

What is the CARE framework?

CARE stands for Context, Action, Result, Example. It's action-oriented and results-driven, and works well for business requests and problem-solving scenarios.

What is the APE framework?

APE stands for Action, Purpose, Expectation. Simple and efficient — best for quick, clear tasks where you just need to define what to do, why, and what the output should look like.

What is the CREATE framework?

CREATE stands for Characterise, Request, Examples, Adjust, Test, Evaluate. A more iterative framework, useful when you want to fine-tune for quality.

What is the TAG framework?

TAG stands for Task, Action, Goal. One of the most straightforward frameworks — great for simple, focused tasks.

What is the CREO framework?

CREO stands for Context, Role, Evidence, Output. Ideal for tasks that require factual accuracy, since it asks for supporting evidence before generating output.

What is the RISE framework?

RISE stands for Role, Input, Steps, Expectation. A reliable general-purpose framework, especially useful for structured, step-by-step output.

What is the PAIN framework?

PAIN stands for Problem, Action, Information, Next Steps. Best for problem-solving prompts, focusing the AI on diagnosing an issue and suggesting what to do next.

What is the COAST framework?

COAST stands for Context, Objective, Actions, Scenario, Task. A comprehensive framework suited for deep, process-heavy tasks with multiple layers of context.

What is the ROSES framework?

ROSES stands for Role, Objective, Scenario, Expected Solution, Steps. One of the more detailed frameworks, breaking a task into a logical sequence — great for complex outputs.

What is the RESEE framework?

RESEE is built around reflection and refinement — best used when you want the AI to evaluate, improve, or build on content that already exists, rather than create from scratch.
