RESEE prompt framework

Review, Evaluate, Suggest, Enhance, Explain — A framework built specifically for improving something you've already written.

When to use RESEE

RESEE is different from the others: it's not for generating new content, it's for reviewing and refining content you already have. Use it for editing drafts, critiquing your own writing, or getting a second opinion on something before you publish or send it.

The RESEE components

R — Review

Ask the AI to read the existing content carefully before responding.

E — Evaluate

Specify what to judge it against — clarity, tone, persuasiveness, accuracy.

S — Suggest

Ask for specific, actionable suggestions rather than vague feedback.

E — Enhance

Ask for an improved version, not just a critique.

E — Explain

Ask the AI to explain why each change helps, so you can apply the lesson elsewhere.

Bad prompt vs. RESEE-structured prompt

Bad prompt:

“Can you check this email?”

Structured with RESEE:

Review: Here is my email to a client about a missed deadline: [paste email].
Evaluate: Judge it on whether it sounds accountable without being overly apologetic.
Suggest: Point out any lines that sound defensive or overly formal.
Enhance: Rewrite it applying your suggestions.
Explain: Briefly explain the two biggest changes you made and why.

What you get back

Specific feedback identifying which lines read as defensive or overly formal, a rewritten version of the email, and a short explanation of the two most impactful changes so the pattern can be reused next time.

Which AI models it works well with

RESEE is especially effective with Claude for longer documents where nuanced tone feedback matters, and works well with ChatGPT for shorter, quicker edits.
