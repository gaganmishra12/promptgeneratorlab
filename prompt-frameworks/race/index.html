RACE prompt framework

Role, Action, Context, Example — A fast, four-part structure for when you need a good prompt in under a minute.

When to use RACE

RACE strips ORACLE down to the essentials. Reach for it when the task is simple enough that you don't need to spell out layout and length separately — quick drafts, short-form content, one-off questions, and situations where you'd rather move fast than be exhaustive.

The RACE components

R — Role

Who the AI should be while it answers — this shapes vocabulary and judgment more than any other single instruction.

A — Action

The exact thing you want done, phrased as a direct instruction rather than a vague ask.

C — Context

Just enough background for the answer to be usable — audience, purpose, or constraints.

E — Example

A short reference point so the AI can match your intended tone or format.

Bad prompt vs. RACE-structured prompt

Bad prompt:

“Give me some tweet ideas.”

Structured with RACE:

Role: You are a social media manager for a bootstrapped SaaS product.
Action: Write five tweet ideas announcing a new feature.
Context: The feature is a dark mode toggle. Our audience is mostly developers who dislike marketing-speak.
Example: Tone should be closer to a changelog entry than an ad — plain, specific, a little dry.

What you get back

Five short tweet drafts, each announcing the feature from a slightly different angle (the feature itself, why it exists, a specific use case, a light joke, a direct CTA), written in the plain, low-hype tone the example called for.

Which AI models it works well with

RACE works well as a universal format, and is especially effective with ChatGPT and Claude for short-form, high-frequency content where speed matters more than exhaustive detail.
